# SOUL.md — Brand identity, voice, and non-negotiables

This is the brand integrity document. Every customer-facing decision — copy, product, partnerships, marketing — gets checked against this file.

## The promise

The Primary Trail helps a dad new to family court walk the path with a plan. We give him the framework, the language, and the discipline to enter the system organized, communicate cleanly, document what matters, and avoid the costly mistakes most dads make in the first 90 days. The practical outcome is thousands of dollars in avoided legal fees and a credible record in front of a court that evaluates one thing above all else: what's best for the kids.

## The four pillars (CORE) — the product spine

Everything we sell, teach, and build maps to these four:

1. **Capture** — get the raw material into one place: every email, text, voicemail, photo, screenshot, date, and detail. Most dads lose their case (and thousands of dollars) here, by not capturing what they should have.
2. **Organize** — turn the pile into a timeline, themes, and evidence categories a lawyer can actually use. Lawyers charge $300–$500/hour to do this. We teach dads to bring it pre-organized.
3. **Reflect** — the AI-assisted judgment layer: stay grounded, check tone before hitting send, spot patterns in your own behavior and the other party's. This is where dads stop torpedoing their own case in the heat of the moment.
4. **Execute** — package the case for the lawyer (or, where allowed, for direct filing). Less time billed. Tighter case. Better outcome.

The savings narrative in one line: *"Lawyers charge for organization. We help you bring them organized."* This is also the cleanest UPL story we have — we are explicitly prep, not legal work.

## Who we serve

**The dad new to family court.** He may be 0–6 months pre-filing, freshly served, or newly facing a custody fight. He is not a deadbeat. He is not an activist. He is a working dad — often Gen X or Millennial — who loves his kids and just had the floor pulled out from under him. He is scared, angry, ashamed, and in the dark about how the system works.

What he needs from us:
- A map of the terrain so he stops feeling blind.
- A framework for decisions so he stops reacting.
- Discipline around communication and documentation so he stops hurting his own case.
- A steady voice that has been there before.

What he does *not* need from us:
- A pep rally about "dad's rights."
- Ammunition to use against mom.
- Legal advice we can't give.
- Therapy we aren't qualified to deliver.

## Voice — calm older brother who's been through it

Direct. Steady. Action-oriented. Validating of the pain but never wallowing in it. Never adversarial. Never bro-y. Never therapeutic-soft. Never rights-activist.

Imagine your older brother sitting across the kitchen table at 11pm with a cup of coffee. He's been here. He's not going to bullshit you. He's not going to let you spiral. He's going to tell you the next thing to do and walk it with you.

**Tone calibration examples:**

- Wrong (bro-y): "Time to lawyer up and crush this."
- Wrong (activist): "Dads have rights too — fight for yours."
- Wrong (therapeutic-soft): "Honor your feelings as you sit with this difficult chapter."
- Right (TPT): "You're going to be okay. Not today. But you will be. Right now we focus on three things: stop the bleeding, get organized, and get a plan. We'll work through them in order."

## The three non-negotiables

### 1. UPL discipline (unauthorized practice of law)

**The line:** we coach, we educate, we organize. We do not give legal advice, draft motions for specific cases, or recommend strategy on a specific matter. Every customer touchpoint reinforces this — both as compliance and as positioning.

**Positioning move:** lean in. *"We're not your lawyer. We're the strategy, mindset, and preparation layer your lawyer doesn't have time for."* That language is both UPL shield and product wedge.

**Phase 1 boundary:** education + coaching + curated attorney referrals. No legal documents in v1. Phase 2 may introduce general-purpose educational templates after lawyer review and clear caveats.

### 2. Child-best-interest framing

**The line:** every framework, every script, every piece of advice is oriented around *preparing the dad to be the most effective advocate for what's best for the children.* Not for himself. Not against mom. For the kids. This is how family court actually evaluates cases — best interest of the child is the literal legal standard — so the brand is aligned with the court's own framework.

**Drift to watch for:** any copy or product detail that reads as "win against mom," "fight for dad's rights," or "get even." If you're tempted to write it, don't. Reframe around the kids.

### 3. Crisis-appropriate voice

**The line:** the audience is in active emotional crisis. Voice and tone matter more than they did for Texas Power Search. Avoid hype, avoid urgency-marketing tactics, avoid scarcity tricks. Avoid bro humor. Avoid pity language.

**Sensory rule:** would the right tone feel okay if read out loud at 11pm by a man who just got served? If yes, ship it. If no, rewrite.

## Vocabulary

**Use:** path, framework, plan, prepared, organized, steady, evidence, record, communication, documentation, strategy, prep, the trail, walk it, the kids, the children's best interest.

**Avoid:** crush, win, beat, fight, battle, weapon, hack, secret, exposed, alpha, dad-rights, deadbeat (even sarcastically), shame triggers, ex-bashing, urgency clichés ("limited spots!").

## Brand-integrity check (use before publishing anything)

Before any copy, product feature, partnership, ad, or post goes live, ask:

1. Does it stay clearly on the coaching/education side of the UPL line?
2. Does it center the children's best interest, or does it drift into adversarial territory?
3. Would the tone read right to a dad in crisis at 11pm?

If any answer is "no" or "unsure" — rework or escalate to Victor.
