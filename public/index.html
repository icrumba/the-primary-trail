WHAT MOST DADS DON'T KNOW

The reality, by the numbers.

The cost of being unprepared lands on you, your kids, and your wallet.

1 in 5 Primary custody

Of custodial parents are fathers.

Roughly 20% of custodial parents are fathers. Texas mirrors the national average. Dads receive about 33% of parenting time — 120 days a year versus 245 for moms.

U.S. Census Bureau via Mims Ballew Hollingsworth Texas Law (2026); CustodyXchange State Data
6–8 days Per month

With their kids during the school year.

Under Texas Standard or Expanded Possession, most non-custodial dads get just 6 to 8 days per month during the school year, plus a 30-day summer block and alternating holidays. The reality is closer to one in four days, not one in two.

Texas Family Code § 153.252 (Standard Possession Order); § 153.254 (Expanded SPO)
Up to 40% Income loss

For men in their 30s the year after divorce.

Men in their 30s lose close to 40% of their income in the year after divorce, versus 9% for women. Across all ages, dads' household income drops 23–25% post-divorce.

Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (2024); U.S. GAO
$5K–$25K Per routine hearing

In legal fees for a single Texas family-court hearing.

A single routine hearing in Texas family court typically runs $5,000 to $25,000 in legal fees. That is not the final trial. That does not include mediation, social studies, custody evaluations, or expert witnesses. Most contested cases have several hearings before trial.

Texas family law firm fee disclosures; ABA fee surveys; Clio Legal Trends Report
$75–$125 One-word reply

What a one-word "yes" to an email costs.

Family law attorneys bill in 15-minute increments at $300–$500 per hour. A one-word "yes" to an email costs $75 to $125. Dads who bring their lawyer a packet — not a pile — save thousands.

Clio Legal Trends Report; ABA fee surveys
Nearly 3x Mental health

Greater odds of dying by suicide for divorced men.

Divorced men have 2.82x greater odds of dying by suicide than married men. For separated men under 34, the odds are eight times higher. Knowledge, preparation, and community change outcomes.

Psychological Bulletin (2025 meta-analysis, 106M+ men); Orygen Research

QUESTIONS

What dads ask.

Are you lawyers?

No. The Primary Trail is a coaching and education service for dads navigating family court. We coach the dad. Your attorney handles the law. We teach dads how to capture what matters, organize it cleanly, communicate without hurting their case, and reflect with honesty. We never draft legal documents, advise on legal strategy, or represent anyone. Any legal decision should be reviewed with a licensed attorney in your jurisdiction.

How is The Primary Trail different from a general divorce coach?

We focus specifically on dads new to family court and on a four-pillar framework: Capture, Organize, Reflect, Execute. The work is built around what family court actually evaluates: the best interest of the children. The dad who shows up to every conversation that matters — with his ex, his kids, his attorney, and himself — is a credible, organized advocate for his kids, not someone fighting his ex.

What does the Primary Trail course cover?

Modules map to the four pillars. Capture: how to collect and timestamp every email, text, voicemail, and document without compromising your case. Organize: building a timeline and evidence categories your lawyer can use. Reflect: tone and judgment work, including AI-assisted message review before you hit send. Execute: packaging your case for your attorney so every billable minute goes to legal work, not coordination work.

Where is The Primary Trail based and can you help dads outside Texas?

The course and community are open to dads anywhere in the United States. Education and coaching are not state-specific. The 1:1 coaching tier launches in Texas first, with in-person availability in Dallas, Collin, and Denton counties. Additional states will be added as the 1:1 program expands.
